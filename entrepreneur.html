RENOVATION OF AFP GYMNASIUM BLDG.

The architectural concept for this college classroom extension building focuses on creating an energy-efficient, forward-thinking academic environment that seamlessly merges passive climate control with modern design. The building’s most defining feature is its series of dramatic, angled concrete fins with triangular geometric perforations. Functioning as structural brise-soleil elements, these fins filter harsh direct sunlight and cast dynamic, scattered shadows across the open-air corridors, naturally cooling the building while reducing eye strain in adjacent classrooms. Warm, natural textures like the vertical wood panels and herringbone-patterned timber on the entrance pylon break the starkness of the industrial gray and white concrete framework, introducing an inviting warmth conducive to collaborative higher education. The building's layout and engineering specifically address its role as an educational facility optimized for student wellness and resource conservation: Flexible Academic Spaces: The linear, modular structure easily accommodates large, high-capacity lecture halls on the ground floor and adaptable seminar rooms on the second level, bordered by wide corridors that can double as informal student breakout areas. Biophilic Integration: Built-in linear planter boxes along the upper balconies integrate greenery directly into the architecture, which helps lower student stress, improves air quality, and enhances overall focus. Rooftop Sustainability: Capitalizing on the building's flat roof footprint, an extensive array of photovoltaic solar panels is paired with rainwater collection tanks. This not only offsets the campus's carbon emissions but serves as a functional, real-world laboratory for sustainable engineering and green management studies.
